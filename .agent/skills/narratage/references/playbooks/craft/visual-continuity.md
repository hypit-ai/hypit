# Visual continuity

- Pick one clearest identity anchor for each recurring person, product, and real location. Reuse it
  in later generated frames instead of redrawing identity from prose.
- A continuous shot group shares one reference frame. Split for duration or a genuine edit; parts
  preserve camera, space, wardrobe, props, and action continuity.
- Define reverse views explicitly. A face-facing selfie and a device-screen shot from that person's
  position cannot use the same background direction; the screen view is the opposite side of the room.
- Keep camera motion small unless the story requires a clear move. Do not introduce a new location,
  camera, or wardrobe inside a locked-reference shot.
- Lock distinctive props in both image and motion prompts. State stable physical invariants and remove
  unnecessary asymmetric props if the model repeatedly duplicates them.
- Only the active speaker talks. A listener remains visibly alive with breathing, eye movement, and
  reaction but no invented dialogue.
- Keep visible text physically attached to its product, screen, document, or authored overlay Track.
