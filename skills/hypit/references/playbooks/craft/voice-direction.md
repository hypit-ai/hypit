# Directing a voice

Read this before choosing, designing or changing a character's voice, including when adapting a
production with an existing Voice Reference. Cast a voice the audience will want to keep hearing.
For creator-led social video, favor a distinctive, engaging presence that makes the words feel
addressed to someone.

## Cast the person through the sound

Imagine who is speaking, whom they are addressing, and what makes their company compelling. A teasing
friend, an infectiously curious guide, a charismatic challenger or a mischievous little creature can
suggest a voice more vividly than a list of acoustic properties. Choose a vocal personality that
belongs to this character and serves the piece's intended appeal.

Give that appeal an audible expression: an inviting warmth, a quick wit, an infectious enthusiasm,
or a confident bite. Energy can come from nimble phrasing, clear emphasis and a responsive attitude.
The strength and shape of the presence should suit the encounter, from a lively recommendation to an
intimate conversation.

Look at the character reference and use the Brief to make the casting choice. A supplied voice can
establish an exact identity. An existing project's voice can continue that identity when its casting
still serves the new piece. For a new person or character, develop the voice that makes this role
worth following. Keep the casting choice and the reference's role in Treatment and Source.

## Compress the voice into a vivid direction

A useful voice description is often one short paragraph: the vocal character and appeal, a few
distinctive sound qualities, and the way they address the listener. Choose qualities that reinforce
one coherent impression. Age range, pitch, accent, texture and diction are useful when they make the
intended voice more specific.

For example:

```text
A magnetic female host with a warm, lightly husky voice, talking to a friend she knows she can win
over. Quick, playful phrasing with crisp emphasis and an audible smile. Conversational American
English, full of curiosity and delighted conviction.
```

A different character can have a different kind of appeal:

```text
A rich, resonant storyteller's voice, warm and conspiratorial, as though sharing a discovery across
a small table. Unhurried British English with precise articulation; curiosity lifts the important
words and a knowing amusement colors the turns in the story.
```

These directions give the voice model a person and a relationship to perform. Preserve the qualities
that define the casting while choosing the language and delivery density the actual work needs.

## Let the sample carry the character

Choose a short, natural line that gives the intended voice room to show itself. A curious question,
an enthusiastic recommendation or a teasing judgment can establish both identity and the manner of
speaking the work calls for. Write the intended pronunciation for unfamiliar names in Script before
using the line as a sample.

Voice Design uses that line to establish the reusable voice. The finished passages receive their
own dialogue and direction. [Voice and performance](voice-and-performance.md#give-a-recurring-person-one-accepted-voice)
owns reference preparation, reuse and the choice between visible A-roll and independent speech;
the selected model's package documentation owns its exact inputs.

## Let one voice express different thoughts

Carry the same vocal identity through the work while letting each passage develop its own attitude.
The speaker may draw someone in, challenge a claim, become delighted by an example, then deliver a
confident verdict. Give those changes to the performance model through the passage's direction.

[Video direction](video-direction.md#direct-the-reason-for-an-action) connects that attitude to gaze,
gesture and interaction. For independent speech, the speech request carries the delivery direction.
Choose time from this intended performance using
[Script measurement](../../creation/script-and-time.md#measure-before-choosing-durations).
The pace estimate sizes the passage; the voice and performance direction give it character.
